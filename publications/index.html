  1. J Gambl Stud
    Online vs. In Person Gambling: Does Exposure to Brick-and-Mortar Gambling Shops Lead to Increased Online Gambling?
    Luning Li, Jing Zhang, and Emmanouil Tranos
    Journal of Gambling Studies, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    As online gambling continues to grow, physical gambling shops remain a prominent feature of our neighbourhoods. We estimate the causal effects of exposure to physical gambling environments on online gambling participation, exploring differences across urban and rural areas, as well as between gaming and betting. Greater exposure to physical gambling shops increases the estimated risk of online gambling: those in the highest exposure decile have approximately 1.49 times the risk of online gambling compared with those in the lowest decile, and the effect is stronger in rural and suburban areas (approximately 1.69 times). Online and land-based gambling are not simply substitutes; a more complex relationship involving both substitution and complementarity exists, depending on the context.

  1. Annals AAG
    Gambling: A Story of Remarkable Stability in Fast-Changing High Streets
    Luning Li and Emmanouil Tranos
    Annals of the American Association of Geographers, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    Despite all the changes that high streets have undergone in Britain, the presence of gambling venues remained remarkably stable between 2014 and 2023. To uncover this, we first built a longitudinal data set of gambling shops in Great Britain during that period, as such data are not readily available. We then employed simple autoregressive models estimated with random forests, predicting annual gambling shop frequencies during 2015 to 2023 using 2014 frequencies as the sole predictor. Our results show a predictive accuracy of 91.8 percent by 2019, which remained robust at 86.2 percent in 2023, even after the disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. This stability lays the foundation for understanding vulnerable groups at risk of gambling-related harms.

  2. GISRUK
    Mobile Phone App Data for Understanding Urban Public Park Usage: Opportunities and Challenges
    Luning Li and Michael Sinclair
    In 33rd Annual GIS Research UK Conference (GISRUK 2025), 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    Using two independent mobile phone app datasets covering three years in the Glasgow City Region, we build a framework to estimate park visitation and map park usage across socio-demographic groups. Preliminary findings highlight the potential of mobile phone app data for providing scalable, highly granular insights into park usage, while also revealing inherent challenges.

  1. Curr Issues Tour
    Identifying tourism attractiveness based on intra-destination tourist behaviour: evidence from Wi-Fi data
    Luning Li, Zixuan Pei, Qiang Li, Fengxin Hao, Xiang Chen, and Jin Chen
    Current Issues in Tourism, 2024
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    This study proposes a stay-time-based tourism attractiveness index (STTAI) grounded in time geography theory, treating locations where tourists frequently stay for long periods as highly attractive. Using Wi-Fi probe request data from about 670,000 travellers over one month in the Shichahai scenic area in Beijing, we map intra-destination attractiveness and use regression analysis to examine how environmental factors relate to it.

  1. Curr Issues Tour
    Space–time tourist flow patterns in community-based tourism: an application of the empirical orthogonal function to Wi-Fi data
    Luning Li, Xiang Chen, Luyun Zhang, Qiang Li, Yang Yang, and Jin Chen
    Current Issues in Tourism, 2023
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    Tracking Wi-Fi probe requests over six months in Beijing’s Shichahai area, this study applies the empirical orthogonal function (EOF) to identify the spatial aggregation and temporal patterns of tourist flows, and then examines how weather, air quality and travel days relate to those patterns. It is among the first studies to employ Wi-Fi data to study travel behaviour in community-based tourism.

  1. TBS
    Contextualizing human dynamics: Understanding the semantics of movement trajectories with Wi-Fi data
    Luning Li, Xiang Chen, Qiang Li, Xiaoyue Tan, Jin Chen, Dawei Wang, and 1 more author
    Travel Behaviour and Society, 2021
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    Using Wi-Fi detectors that capture track points along travellers’ trajectories, this study proposes a semantic space–time matrix method to infer the meaning of movement. Applied to the Shichahai scenic area in Beijing with Wi-Fi data from 463,874 individuals over two weeks, the derived semantics were validated against a field survey.

  2. IJGI
    Pedestrian flow prediction in open public places using graph convolutional network
    Menghang Liu, Luning Li, Qiang Li, Yu Bai, and Cheng Hu
    ISPRS International Journal of Geo-Information, 2021
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    We apply a graph convolutional network (GCN) and its spatio-temporal extension (STGCN) to predict crowd flow on a walking street. The models consistently outperform five baselines (HA, ARIMA, SVM, CNN and LSTM) on RMSE, MAE and R-squared, performing best on weekends and at peak hours.
