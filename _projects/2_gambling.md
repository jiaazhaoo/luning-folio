**University of Bristol & University of Glasgow · 2023–present · Bristol Hub for Gambling Harms Research**

Many people think high-street betting shops are disappearing as gambling moves online. This project tests that idea with spatial data and asks which social and spatial inequalities make gambling harms worse.

- **A new longitudinal dataset.** We built a 2014–2023 dataset of gambling shops in Great Britain from points-of-interest data. No such dataset was readily available before.
- **Remarkable stability.** Autoregressive random-forest models that use only 2014 counts predict gambling-shop frequencies with **91.8% accuracy by 2019** and **86.2% in 2023**, even after COVID-19.
- **Exposure and online gambling.** People in the highest decile of exposure to gambling shops have about **1.49 times** the risk of gambling online compared with the lowest decile, rising to about **1.69 times** in rural and suburban areas. Online and land-based gambling are not simple substitutes.

Together these results show that physical gambling environments still matter for place-based policy aimed at reducing gambling-related harms.
